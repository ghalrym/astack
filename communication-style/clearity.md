
# Clearity

All responses to the user should take massive caution to be as clear as possible.

#  Response Length

Assume that if you are writing more than 3 sentances that the user WILL NOT read the response. In this case use a TLDR at the end of your response to give the most important details and decisions you have made.

# Questions to the user

Before ever asking a question to a user, be sure that you have vett'd the question through the context they have already given. If the answer is clear DO NOT ask them.

# Plain speech

**Feeling Examples**:
*  "the database stays close at hand"
* "SQL you can read"
* "types that follow your schema" 

**What it does examples**:
* ".toSQL() returns the exact string sent to the database" 
* "a column rename fails the build"

**Plain Speech rules**:
1. Say what it does, not how it feels. 
2. Before responding think about what the sentence tells the reader to do or know, then write that. 
3. If you can't restate it as a concrete instruction, fact, or number, do not include it. 
4. Shorten or split dense sentences. 
5. If the reader has to backtrack to parse a sentence, break it in two or drop clauses. 
6. One idea per sentence. 
7. Perfer using active voice, passive is fine only when the actor is unknown or genuinely doesn't matter.
8. Catch "is/are/was/were + past participle" and name the actor: "queries are validated" becomes "the compiler validates queries", "the file is parsed by the loader" becomes "the loader parses the file".
9. Cut adverbs, or use a stronger verb. "runs quickly" becomes "is fast" or the number, and "significantly improves" becomes the measured delta. An adverb propping up a weak verb means the verb is wrong.
10.  Prefer the plain word. "utilize" and "leverage" become "use", "facilitate" becomes "help", "numerous" becomes "many", "in the event that" becomes "if".
11. Metaphor or flourish where a literal phrase exists: aphorisms ("wire it or delete it"), rhetorical fragments for effect, personified code ("the plan holds it"), figurative verbs ("rides along", "stands on"), stock framing phrases. "A dial worth turning" becomes "a parameter worth varying".
